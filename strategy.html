Nosebleed CapitalStrategy

The whole trade: get in at face value through a channel most buyers can't use, get out on the open resale market once real demand is priced in.

The core trade

Buy in the primary market at face value. Sell in the secondary market once scarcity is priced in. The edge isn't taste or luck — it's access. Most tickets never touch resale at a premium because most buyers pay whatever the primary market charges, at whatever time slot they can get. The trade only works when there's a legitimate way to buy before, or instead of, the general public — and a legitimate way to resell after.

Primary-side access channels

  • Amex Presale Tickets®. Early access before general on-sale — this is the actual mechanism behind the US Open trade: buying before the public even can, ahead of demand getting priced in. Platinum, not Centurion, is what unlocks this. Centurion adds concierge-sourced access to separate, already-scarce VIP inventory at a $10k init + $5k/yr cost; it's not a better version of the same presale and isn't worth carrying for this strategy's margins.
  • Amex Reserved Tickets® — a distinct, weaker tier. This is a dedicated allotment reserved for Card Members, not necessarily an early window ahead of the public. It's an exclusive channel, not inherently a mispricing window, and it's what most Amex-branded corporate hospitality (e.g. F1 Paddock Club) actually is. Don't assume the presale thesis applies just because the badge says "Amex" — check whether it's actually a pre-general-on-sale window or just a walled-off allotment.
  • Premium Events Collection™ — curated, bespoke experiences (dining, entertainment, fashion, sports) for Platinum and Centurion members. Not resellable tickets. Out of scope for this strategy entirely.
  • Official lotteries / ballots. Masters badges, Wimbledon's public ballot, and some Ryder Cup allocations are lottery-based rather than first-come presales. These get face-value access without needing any card affiliation — but check the resale terms before assuming a ballot win is tradeable (e.g. Masters badges cannot legally be resold).
  • Cardmember/loyalty on-sale windows from the venue, league, or ticketing platform directly (e.g. season-ticket-holder presales, team credit union or bank partnerships) — same shape as Amex, different gatekeeper.

Secondary-side exit

Sell through an official or verified resale channel (e.g. Ticketmaster Verified Resale) rather than an informal one, and price off sold comps, not asking prices. Two numbers matter and they are not the same thing: the seller's take-home price and the buyer's paid price differ by the platform's fee stack (e.g. Ticketmaster: buyer pays seller price × ~1.165, seller keeps seller price × 0.90). Set the listing price by working backward from a buyer-facing comp, not by copying it.
